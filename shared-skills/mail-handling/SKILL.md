# Mail handling

How to work out what a person wants done with their mail, agree it with them, and reduce
what they see without ever hiding something on an assumption. Applies to any profile that
touches a mailbox: an admin or PA supporting a team, or an executive assistant supporting one
person. Triage mechanics and draft-reply safety live in `email-inbox-triage`; this is the
behaviour around them.

## When to Use

- A new profile has just been set up and mail access works.
- The person says the inbox is noisy, or asks you to stop showing them something.
- Before any decision to filter, mute, file or hide a category of mail.

## Infer first

Reading a mailbox shows what someone *did*, never what they value. Take these as
observations, not conclusions:

- Senders they never answer: newsletters, system notices, receipts, automated calendar mail.
- Senders they always answer, and senders they answer slowly.
- Whether folders, categories or flags are actually in use, or vestigial.
- Their voice, from sent mail to the same recipient type.
- Their real urgency, from response latency rather than from any definition they give you.

## Say it as a hypothesis

Never install a rule quietly. Say what you think and leave the decision with them:

> "I think newsletters and system mail are noise for you, and that drafts are only worth it
> where a real person is waiting. Both stay visible until you tell me otherwise."

Being wrong out loud is cheap; being wrong quietly removes something they needed.

## Curve visibility, never cut it

| Step | Looks like | Allowed when |
|---|---|---|
| Keep | Listed in full | The default for anything unconfirmed |
| Group | One line with a count, full list on request | They agree the category is low value |
| Divert | Moved to a folder or digest, announced when it happens | They have seen the grouped version and not asked for it |
| Hide | Not shown at all | An explicit instruction only |

One step at a time, announced, with the undo offered in the same breath: "I've started
grouping supplier newsletters, say the word and they come back." Leave weeks between steps,
because a medium-importance thread needs a chance to prove itself before it disappears.

**Nothing at medium importance should vanish because it was dull in March.** A slow reply to
a supplier is not evidence the supplier does not matter, and silence on a newsletter is not a
request to remove it.

## Agree permissions with an example

Filing, flagging, categorising and automating are agreed with a concrete message on screen,
never asked cold:

> "Here is a thread I would have filed under X. Want me to do that as I go, or leave it?"

A permission asked in the abstract gets either a refusal or a rule nobody meant. Record each
grant with its date and exact scope in the person's context file, because permission to file
supplier receipts is not a licence to reorganise the inbox.

Introduce automation only after the manual version has been done correctly in front of them
twice.

## Record where it will be read later

- The person's context file: the hypothesis they confirmed or corrected, the grants with
  dates and scope, and anything they said about specific senders.
- Never in the setup document: that is read once, and this behaviour is needed daily.

## Pitfalls

- **Treating low reply rates as consent.** The most common wrong turn, and the one that loses
  mail the person cared about.
- **Filtering on day one.** You do not yet know which quiet thread is load-bearing.
- **Asking for blanket permission.** "Can I manage your inbox?" gets a yes and means nothing;
  ask about one action at a time.
- **Recording a grant in chat.** If it is not in the context file, the next session will ask
  again or, worse, assume.
- **Letting the ladder run backwards silently.** If something hidden becomes relevant again,
  bring it back and say so.

## Verification

- Every reduction in visibility is one step, announced, and reversible on request.
- Each permission in the context file names the action, the scope and the date.
- A month later, the person can still find anything you grouped or diverted, through you.
