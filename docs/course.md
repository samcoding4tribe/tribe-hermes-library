# A short course for a new user

Seven lessons, about an hour in total. Written for someone who has never used an
agent before. Each lesson has one thing to do and one thing to check.

## What you are getting

An assistant that works on this machine, in its own folder, with its own memory.
It drafts, checks and looks things up. It does not send email, book travel or move
money. Those stay with you, on purpose.

It does not know anything until you tell it or show it, and it says so when it
does not know.

## Lesson 1: start it (5 minutes)

**Do:** open the app and pick your profile, or run `admin` in a terminal. You are
in a new session.

**Ask:** "Who are you and what is your first rule?"

**Check:** it names your role and says it drafts but does not send. If it answers
as a generic assistant, the profile did not load.

## Lesson 2: give it real work (10 minutes)

**Do:** paste two or three emails and ask for one reply, drafted.

**Ask:** "Draft a reply to the second one, short, and tell me what you left out."

**Check:** you get a draft and a reason. If the draft is wrong, do not rewrite it
yourself. Say what is wrong in plain words: "too formal, and you missed that she
needs the numbers by Thursday." Watch it correct.

## Lesson 3: teach it a fact (10 minutes)

**Do:** tell it something durable about your work: "Remember that supplier
invoices go to accounts, and that Rashid at the venue is the only person who can
confirm the room."

**Check:** ask it later in the same conversation what it knows about invoices. Then
ask it to show you what it has remembered. If a fact is wrong, say "forget that"
and give the right one.

Memory is short on purpose. Dates and one-off details belong in the files, not in
its head.

## Lesson 4: how it learns a procedure (15 minutes)

**Do:** walk it through one job, step by step, while it follows along. Then do the
same job a second time and mention the shortcut you use.

**Ask:** "Write that down as a procedure for next time."

**Check:** read what it wrote before you trust it. If a step is wrong, correct it
now. A wrong procedure repeats forever.

## Lesson 5: what it will not do (5 minutes)

**Do:** ask it to send an email. It should refuse and offer a draft instead. Ask it
to delete a folder. It should ask you first, with a card that has approve and deny.

**Check:** you understand that a yes is per action, not per day.

Never type a password into the chat. If a site needs a login, sign in inside the
preview pane and it will work from that session.

## Lesson 6: where your things live (5 minutes)

- Your assistant runs from a folder on this machine.
- Every evening it copies its soul, memory and session history to your own private
  OneDrive folder. Nobody else reads that folder.
- Templates and shared skills live in the shared library. Your memory never goes
  there.

**Check:** open your OneDrive folder and see `text/` and `db/`. That is your
backup.

## Lesson 7: when it gets something wrong (10 minutes)

Say what is wrong, plainly, in the same conversation. Then say what should happen
next time. If it wrote a procedure or a memory note, ask it to fix that too.

If something is properly broken, the nightly history on the maintaining machine
can put a previous version back. Tell whoever looks after your machine what you
saw and when.

## Before you use it daily

- [ ] It answers to your name and role
- [ ] You have corrected it at least once and seen it take the correction
- [ ] You know it will not send anything without you
- [ ] You know where your backup is
- [ ] You know who to tell when it breaks

## Three rules of thumb

1. Ask for a draft, not for permission to act. It is faster and safer.
2. Short, specific corrections work better than rewriting its output.
3. If you would not put it in an email to a colleague, do not put it in memory.
