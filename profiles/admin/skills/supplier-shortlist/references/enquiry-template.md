# The enquiry, as a reusable question set

Copy this, adapt the bracketed parts, keep the numbering — identical structure across
suppliers is what makes the answers comparable. Send as a plain email from the owner's
account, not as an attachment.

## Opening paragraph

> I'm the [role] at [company], a [size] [sector] business in [city]. I'm evaluating
> [capability] for [the reason, stated plainly — e.g. "so that no client data leaves the
> country and nothing is retained or trained on"].

Name the reason. Suppliers price differently for a compliance requirement than for a
convenience one, and it is the line that gets a serious answer.

## The numbered questions

1. **What exactly you provide.** The specific models, SKUs, versions or tiers served
   today, with the exact checkpoint or product code, and how quickly new releases land.
2. **Where it physically runs.** Data centre and city, and whether any part of the
   request path — logging, telemetry, support access, backups — leaves the country.
3. **Retention and training.** What is stored, for how long, who can see it, and
   whether our inputs are used for training or evaluation. In writing.
4. **Limits and capacity.** The throughput or quota we can actually draw, what happens
   at the ceiling, and whether a paid tier changes it.
5. **Price in AUD.** Every unit rate, any monthly minimum, platform fee or seat charge,
   overage treatment, and the cost of a burst month at our volumes.
6. **Change policy.** Who decides when a version, tier or price is upgraded or retired,
   and how much notice we get. Silent upgrades break working setups.
7. **Security and compliance.** Current certifications with dates (ISO 27001, IRAP
   level, hosting-framework status, SOC 2), and whether they will sign our terms
   rather than theirs.
8. **Exit.** How our data, logs and any configuration are deleted at the end, in what
   timeframe, and what proof we get.

## The trial ask

> A two-week trial at realistic volume — [n units] over the period — would let us test
> against our own inputs rather than a demo.

State the volume. "As much as we need" gets a sandbox too small to learn anything from.

## Follow-up when the answer is partial

- Missing retention or deletion terms: that is the answer to chase first, because the
  rest of the comparison reduces to price otherwise.
- Price without a volume assumption: reply with the assumed volume in one line and ask
  for the figure at that level.
- "Talk to sales": fine, book it, but ask for the written answers first so the call is
  about the gaps rather than the basics.
